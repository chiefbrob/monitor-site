What a reviewer should look for

The microphone is actually in use. The level meter moves, the Android privacy indicator shows in the status bar, and the pinned notification stays up while the service runs.

It is user-initiated. Nothing starts until the user presses Start and accepts the disclosure and the Android prompt.

The alert is the task. When the level holds above the trigger, the phone shows the alert and the call path. A camera failure or an interrupted service never hides that the monitor was interrupted.

Use case

Background audio input

App functionality

The core feature is continuous room-noise monitoring. After an in-app microphone disclosure and the Android permission prompt, the user presses Start. A microphone foreground service measures the room's loudness in memory and compares it with the threshold the user set. If the level stays above the threshold for the hold time, the phone shows an alert and places a call to the user's number. Audio is measured as dBFS and discarded; it is never recorded, saved or uploaded. The screen may be off or the app in the background, and a visible ongoing notification is always shown while the service runs.

User impact if interrupted

If Android defers or interrupts the microphone foreground service, the room is not monitored: no alert is raised and no call is placed. The app shows “monitoring was interrupted” rather than pretending to listen, and the user must press Start again. No audio is buffered or replayed when it resumes.
