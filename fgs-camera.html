What a reviewer should look for

The camera is actually in use. Live frames play in the app's Watch screen, and Android shows the camera privacy indicator while the viewer is connected.

It is user-initiated. The camera starts only after the user switches Live view on, enables video, and accepts the camera disclosure. It is never on for an empty room.

It stops by itself. The camera capturer starts on the first viewer and stops when the last viewer leaves; the monitor keeps its microphone service.

The service is visible. The ongoing notification and the privacy chip stay in the status bar while the camera runs.

Background camera streaming

App functionality

Baby Monitor is a baby monitor. The user can switch on Include video (Live view) and/or Remote photos. With Live view, this phone publishes the room camera over a direct WebRTC connection to a second phone the user runs on the same Wi-Fi, while this phone's screen is off or the app is in the background. With Remote photos, one phone the user paired by QR can request a single still photo over the user's own Tailscale network; the camera opens only for that request and closes immediately. The camera is never used unless the user has switched one of these features on, and the ongoing foreground-service notification is always visible.

User impact if interrupted

If Android defers or interrupts the camera foreground service, a second phone cannot start watching and a remote-photo request fails after a short timeout. The app reports the failure in the Live view card or the remote viewer and keeps the noise monitor, alert and call running. A camera failure never stops monitoring or the call path.
